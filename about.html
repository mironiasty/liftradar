About the data

Sources

Every source, and what it gives, is listed on Data sources; each file's sources lists the URLs used for that report. The Snow Card, SalzburgerLand and Kärnten pages show the report each resort submits to iSKI; holidayinfo.cz is where Czech resorts submit theirs (Sitour CZ, part of the iSKI network), Lumiplan where French resorts do (Alpe d'Huez uses ATEN Altitude instead). Not covered yet in France: Avoriaz, Val Cenis, Montgenèvre, Saint-Lary, Font-Romeu, Ax 3 Domaines, Auron, Orcières, Chamrousse and Les Orres (no source we can read).

Timing

  • Updated twice a day, at about 07:40 and 12:40 Vienna time in winter (08:40 / 13:40 in summer time). Scheduled runs can start late at busy times, sometimes by hours, and one can be skipped.
  • fetched_at is when we read the data; lifts_updated_at is the resort's own report time (or its slope map's update time; null for French resorts, whose sources have none). The resort's report time can move forward without its numbers changing: reporting systems refresh it daily.
  • Times are ISO 8601 with the Vienna offset (+01:00 / +02:00, the same as Swiss time); archive file names are UTC.

Status

  • Open / closed (open / closed): from the resort's report, or open when its slope map shows open slopes. Swiss resorts are open when infosnow (or the slope map) shows an open slope: lifts also run for hikers outside the season. French resorts likewise, from Lumiplan; a lift or slope “scheduled” to open later that day (as the 07:40 run sees them) counts as open, as in Lumiplan's own totals. Some French resorts show everything closed overnight, so they can read closed in the morning and open at midday. Alpe d'Huez (ATEN Altitude) follows the same rules; in October 2026 its page still showed a chairlift and two slopes as open, so it reads open until its season data replaces them.
  • No report yet (no_report): the resort hasn't reported for 14 days (e.g. before the season). Only the totals of its last report (lifts, slopes, km: the area's size) are kept, with 0 open lifts and slopes.open null, so last season's open counts and snow never show up as current. Where the report lists every lift and slope, the totals of lifts_by_type and slopes_by_difficulty are kept the same way. The 0 means “not reported”, not that every lift is closed. Some Slovak and Polish areas (TMR's, Kotelnica, Czarna Góra, Szrenica, Karpacz, Pilsko; Zieleniec while its site refuses us) are always “no report yet” for now: we list them with their size only, from the operators' lists, until we read a live status for them. French resorts are “no report yet” while all their slopes are “hors période” (outside the season), with the area's totals; before the season many French bulletins list nothing, and then there are no totals either.
  • Unknown (null): usually because fetching failed; see errors.
  • A closed resort always shows 0 open lifts, slopes and km (outside the season some resorts count lifts running for hikers), and no snow depths (mountain_cm, valley_cm, fresh_cm, condition are null): closed resorts' reports typically still carry last season's depths. last_snowfall and station measurements are kept.

Snow

  • Mountain / valley (mountain_cm / valley_cm): the highest / lowest measuring station (Hintertux, Sölden, Kitzsteinhorn, Kaunertal, Pitztal, from their own websites), or the report's max / min depth (Snow Card, SalzburgerLand, holidayinfo: depth on the slopes).
  • PKL and Zieleniec: the lowest / highest snow cover of the open slopes.
  • French resorts: the measuring points of the resort's Lumiplan bulletin (e.g. “Col Balme”, 2500 m), each with depth and snow quality (condition, e.g. Douce); fresh snow is the last snowfall's amount when it fell today or yesterday. Resorts without a bulletin (Sainte Foy, Valmorel, Les Contamines, Les 2 Alpes, Les 7 Laux, Le Collet) have no snow depth. Alpe d'Huez: the measuring points of its ATEN Altitude page (e.g. “Pic Blanc”, 3300 m), the same way.
  • condition is in the source's language (e.g. griffig, technický).
  • Swiss resorts: the resort's own measuring points where it enters them in infosnow (e.g. Verbier). Most don't, and then the depths come from the nearest SLF stations, listed in snow.stations as “<name> (SLF)”: an automatic station on the mountain (about 2000–2900 m) and an observer's study plot near the village (read each morning, mostly from November). They're measured on open ground, not on pistes, and the station can be a few km from the ski area.
  • Regional reports keep a resort's old values until it clears them; check last_snowfall. SalzburgerLand reports often have no last_snowfall.
  • Any value can be null when the resort doesn't publish it.

Lifts and slopes

  • One ski area is one resort. The reports list some areas per village, each with the whole area's lift and slope counts (SkiWelt, KitzSki, Zillertal Arena, Mayrhofen, Ski Juwel, Snow Space Salzburg, Hochkönig, Dachstein West, Großarltal - Dorfgastein): they're published once, under the area's name, from the village report updated last. Snow depths are that village's.
  • Total slope km (slopes.total_km) is the ski area's size for all but about 20 resorts: the size advertised on the resort's own site, otherwise OnTheSnow's or skiresort.com's figure. Sectors of a linked area with no figure of their own (St. Anton, Lech Zürs and Warth-Schröcken; Gstaad, Lenzerheide, Verbier, Thyon, Champéry, Morgins; Val di Fassa, Val di Fiemme) get the sum of the operator's own slope lengths, ski routes included where the map has them; slopes without a length (mostly links and practice slopes) are left out, so these can be a little low. For the rest it's the reported value, which is only the sum of the slope lengths the resort has entered in its reporting system and can be far too low (Kitzbühel reported 4.7 of 233 km). Advertised sizes are counted in different ways, some with ski routes, and Stubai's 108 km is its “effective route distance” (65 km by fall line). Rounded up to a whole km. Not covered yet: Megève, Crest-Voland, Notre-Dame-de-Bellecombe, Valloire, Valmeinier, La Thuile, Val d'Allos, Samoëns and Autrans-Méaudre km, La Rosière and Queyras, and a few village lifts nobody publishes a size for.
  • Open km (slopes.open_km, 0.1 km): summed from the slope map's lengths where complete; from the resort's live infobox (Hintertux); from OpenStreetMap lengths of the open slopes (Kitzsteinhorn, Kaunertal), which leans low because OSM lines are shorter than official lengths; otherwise as reported.
  • SalzburgerLand reports and the Trentino bulletin have slope km but no slope counts: the slope total comes from the area's size, and open slopes stay null (0 when closed). A few small areas (Planai, Dachstein Gletscher, Feuerkogel, Uttendorf and some village lifts) still have no slope total.
  • Lift and slope totals from different sources don't always match the advertised ones: slope maps include practice lifts and magic carpets (the SkiWelt map lists 147 lifts, the area advertises 81).

Lift types and slope difficulties

lifts_by_type and slopes_by_difficulty are only set for resorts with their own slope map (the bigger areas), for Swiss resorts (from infosnow), for Czech resorts (from holidayinfo), for French resorts (from Lumiplan) and for the Polish ones (PKL, Zieleniec). Some bigger areas without one (Hochkönig, Snow Space Salzburg, Schmittenhöhe, Zauchensee, Serre Chevalier, ...) get slopes_by_difficulty from the area's size instead: km per difficulty (skiresort.com), or slope counts (Paganella, Folgaria), with open null (0 when closed). Ski Arlberg's breakdowns get their km per difficulty the same way, summed from its slope map.

  • Lift types (gondola, cable_car, chairlift, drag_lift, carpet, funicular, other) and difficulties (easy, intermediate, difficult, ski_route, other) are read from the map's display text, so an unusual label can land in other. For Swiss resorts they come from infosnow's type icons; yellow and orange runs are ski_route, mixed colours (blue/red) other, and rack railways (e.g. Gornergrat) other. For Czech resorts they come from holidayinfo's codes: two-colour and white slopes are other; a lift or slope open only on some days (e.g. Wednesday to Sunday) counts as open on those days. Polish slopes follow the Polish colours: “łatwa” (blue) easy, PKL's “trudna” and Zieleniec's “średnia” (red) intermediate, “bardzo trudna” / Zieleniec's “trudna” (black) difficult. French slopes follow the French colours: green (very easy) and blue are both easy, red intermediate, black difficult; only alpine slopes count (no snowparks, cross-country or sledding).
  • km per difficulty are only given when every slope of that difficulty has a length in the map, and they don't add up to the total slope km (the official size, counted differently; each difficulty is also rounded up). Some maps have no lengths at all.
  • A map not updated for 14 days is ignored. Maps covering several of our resorts (Gastein, Salzburger Sportwelt) aren't used, since they don't say which area a lift belongs to.

Avalanche danger

  • Given for Tirol, Swiss and Italian resorts; Czech resorts in Krkonoše and Jeseníky (the only regions the Czech Mountain Rescue rates; other Czech areas have none); Kasprowy Wierch (TOPR, the Polish Tatras; the other Polish areas have no bulletin); French resorts in the Alps and Pyrenees (Météo-France, by massif, e.g. Vanoise; the Jura, Vosges and Massif Central have none); and Jasná, Tatranská Lomnica and Štrbské Pleso (Slovak HZS; found from a point in the ski area, since the villages lie below the warning regions). Salzburg and the other Austrian states have no machine-readable bulletin, so avalanche is null for all resorts there (including Lech Zürs and Warth-Schröcken in Vorarlberg), and the Snow Card ones in Salzburg (Hochkrimml, Königsleiten, Mittersill, Wildkogel).
  • Early and late in the season the SLF only rates the alpine regions, so Swiss resorts in the Prealps (and e.g. Arosa) can have none while others have a rating.
  • null outside the season and when no bulletin is valid at the time of the run.
  • The level (danger_level, 1–5) is the highest of the day in the resort's warning region; ratings has the split by elevation and time of day. The region is found from one point per resort, so a resort spanning two regions shows only one.

Locations

location is one point per resort: the Snow Card or infosnow location, a point in the ski area for the glacier resorts, or the main village or ski station (looked up on OpenStreetMap) for other Austrian, Italian, French, Czech, Slovak and Polish resorts, Andermatt and Engelberg. It marks the area on a map, not a lift station.

region is the state, canton, Italian region, Czech kraj, Polish voivodeship or French département that point lies in (e.g. Tirol, Valais, Valle d'Aosta, Liberecký kraj, Žilinský kraj, Małopolskie, Savoie; Trentino-Alto Adige by province: Südtirol, Trentino), looked up on OpenStreetMap; the table filters by it and by country. An area spanning a border is listed under one of them (Katschberg under Kärnten, Glacier 3000 under Vaud).

Areas on the map

The map shows the outline of 295 of the 422 resorts, from OpenStreetMap's ski areas (landuse=winter_sports); the others, mostly small village lifts, are only a marker. The French resorts have no outlines yet. An area within 1 km of several resorts is split between them by nearest resort point, so their parts are approximate. The outlines are updated now and then, not with every data update.

Archive

  • Collected since 24 October 2026; earlier data doesn't exist.
  • A snapshot is only written when a report changed (apart from fetched_at), and the archive index (archive/<country>/<resort>/index.json) lists every run. Snapshots keep the format of their time, so older ones may lack newer fields.

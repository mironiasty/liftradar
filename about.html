Sources

Every source, and what it gives, is listed on Data sources; each file's sources lists the URLs used for that report. The Snow Card, SalzburgerLand and Kärnten pages show the report each resort submits to iSKI; holidayinfo.cz is where Czech resorts submit theirs (Sitour CZ, part of the iSKI network), Lumiplan where French resorts do (Alpe d'Huez uses ATEN Altitude instead). Not covered yet in France: Avoriaz, Val Cenis, Montgenèvre, Saint-Lary, Font-Romeu, Ax 3 Domaines, Auron, Orcières, Chamrousse and Les Orres (no source we can read). Finnish resorts come from ski.fi, the Finnish Ski Area Association's site, where each resort fills in a daily block, or from the resort's own page or slope map where it shows a live status. Not covered yet in Finland: Levi's own lift list (its ski.fi block is used), Paljakka, Koli and the smaller hills. German resorts in the Sauerland come from the lift overview of the Wintersport-Arena Sauerland, the lift operators' association, and the bigger Bavarian and Saxon areas from their slope maps. Not covered yet in Germany: a live status for the other 83 areas (the Allgäu, most of Upper Bavaria, the Black Forest, the Harz, Saxony, Thuringia), which are listed with “no report” for now; Outdooractive, where many of them enter their snow report, allows private use only. Nor St. Englmar, Mitterfirmiansreut and other Bavarian Forest areas, Wasserkuppe, Erbeskopf, Hoherodskopf and single-lift areas.

Timing

  • Updated twice a day, at about 07:40 and 12:40 Vienna time in winter (08:40 / 13:40 in summer time). Scheduled runs can start late at busy times, sometimes by hours, and one can be skipped.
  • fetched_at is when we read the data; lifts_updated_at is the resort's own report time (or its slope map's update time; null for French, Finnish and German resorts, whose sources have none: ski.fi dates its blocks by the day only, the Wintersport-Arena not at all). The resort's report time can move forward without its numbers changing: reporting systems refresh it daily. Slope maps (Intermaps) instead keep it until a lift or slope opens or closes, so a resort that opens the same lifts every day can show a report time days old although we read it in the latest run.
  • The map and the resort pages show “Checked” (when we last read the report: fetched_at, or status_from when the latest run couldn't read it) and the resort's last change (lifts_updated_at). Both are highlighted for an open resort whose report the latest run couldn't read, or whose report time is more than 7 days old.
  • Times are ISO 8601 with the Vienna offset (+01:00 / +02:00, the same as Swiss time); archive file names are UTC.

Status

  • Open / closed (open / closed): from the resort's report, or open when its slope map shows open slopes. Swiss resorts are open when infosnow (or the slope map) shows an open slope: lifts also run for hikers outside the season. French resorts likewise, from Lumiplan; a lift or slope “scheduled” to open later that day (as the 07:40 run sees them) counts as open, as in Lumiplan's own totals. Some French resorts show everything closed overnight, so they can read closed in the morning and open at midday. Alpe d'Huez (ATEN Altitude) follows the same rules; in October 2026 its page still showed a chairlift and two slopes as open, so it reads open until its season data replaces them.
  • Finnish resorts: from the resort's own counters, slope list or slope map where it has one (Ylläs, Ruka, Tahko, Himos, Salla, Ukkohalla, Iso-Syöte, Vuokatti, Laajis, Messilä, Ounasvaara, Riihivuori, Suomutunturi, Kasurila), open when a slope is, with no report time, so always taken as current. The others come from the block they fill in on ski.fi (open slopes and lifts, and the day it was updated): open when a slope is open, “no report yet” when the block is more than 14 days old, and closed when ski.fi's list of resorts flags the resort closed, even if its block still counts open slopes.
  • German resorts: a Sauerland area is open when the Wintersport-Arena's lift icon says so, lifts are open and its snow depth, where it gives one, isn't 0. It has no report time, so its open/closed is always taken as current. Where the resort's slope map is current (Winterberg, Sudelfeld, Brauneck, Spitzingsee, Großer Arber, Ofterschwang, Bolsterlang, Fichtelberg, Zugspitze, Hohenbogen), it's open when a slope on the map is, and “no report” when the map is more than 14 days old. The other German areas have no source yet and are always “no report”.
  • No report (no_report): the resort hasn't reported for 14 days (e.g. before the season), or we couldn't read its report for over 2 days (see below). Only the totals of its last report (lifts, slopes, km: the area's size) are kept, with 0 open lifts and slopes.open null, so last season's open counts and snow never show up as current. Where the report lists every lift and slope, the totals of lifts_by_type and slopes_by_difficulty are kept the same way. The 0 means “not reported”, not that every lift is closed. Some Slovak and Polish areas (TMR's, Kotelnica, Czarna Góra, Szrenica, Karpacz, Pilsko; Zieleniec while its site refuses us) are always “no report” for now: we list them with their size only, from the operators' lists, until we read a live status for them. French resorts are “no report” while all their slopes are “hors période” (outside the season), with the area's totals; before the season many French bulletins list nothing, and then there are no totals either.
  • The status is never null. When a run can't read a resort's report (its site is down or changed), the resort keeps the status, lifts and slopes of the last run that could, and status_from gives that run's time (it's null when the report was read this time). After 2 days without a report it turns “no report”, with only the area's size kept. What failed is in each run's log, kept for 30 days in logs/ on the data branch. Reports still list it in errors, which the site doesn't show; that field is deprecated (optional in the schema) and will be removed.
  • A closed resort always shows 0 open lifts, slopes and km (outside the season some resorts count lifts running for hikers), and no snow depths (mountain_cm, valley_cm, fresh_cm, condition are null): closed resorts' reports typically still carry last season's depths. last_snowfall and station measurements are kept.

Snow

  • Mountain / valley (mountain_cm / valley_cm): the report's max / min depth (Snow Card, SalzburgerLand, holidayinfo: depth on the slopes), or the highest / lowest measuring station where a resort publishes its stations (Hintertux, Sölden, Kitzsteinhorn, Kaunertal, Pitztal).
  • PKL and Zieleniec: the lowest / highest snow cover of the open slopes.
  • French resorts: the measuring points of the resort's Lumiplan bulletin (e.g. “Col Balme”, 2500 m), each with depth and snow quality (condition, e.g. Douce); fresh snow is the last snowfall's amount when it fell today or yesterday. Resorts without a bulletin (Sainte Foy, Valmorel, Les Contamines, Les 2 Alpes, Les 7 Laux, Le Collet) have no snow depth. Alpe d'Huez: the measuring points of its ATEN Altitude page (e.g. “Pic Blanc”, 3300 m), the same way.
  • condition is in the source's language (e.g. griffig, technický).
  • Swiss resorts: the resort's own measuring points where it enters them in infosnow (e.g. Verbier). Most don't, and then the depths come from the nearest SLF stations, listed in snow.stations as “<name> (SLF)”: an automatic station on the mountain (about 2000–2900 m) and an observer's study plot near the village (read each morning, mostly from November). They're measured on open ground, not on pistes, and the station can be a few km from the ski area.
  • Finnish resorts: the depth at the nearest weather station of the Finnish Meteorological Institute that measures snow (within 40 km), as valley_cm and a station “<name> (FMI)”: natural snow on open ground in the lowlands, often an airport or village, not on the slopes (Häkärinteet and Suomutunturi have none nearby). Ruka's natural snow on the fell, from its slope map, is mountain_cm. No resort reports its piste depth.
  • German resorts: the one depth each Sauerland area enters on the Wintersport-Arena's overview, as mountain_cm (no measuring stations, no new snow or condition). Resorts read from their slope maps have no snow depth.
  • Regional reports keep a resort's old values until it clears them; check last_snowfall. SalzburgerLand reports often have no last_snowfall.
  • Any value can be null when the resort doesn't publish it.

Lifts and slopes

  • One ski area is one resort. The reports list some areas per village, each with the whole area's lift and slope counts (SkiWelt, KitzSki, Zillertal Arena, Mayrhofen, Ski Juwel, Snow Space Salzburg, Hochkönig, Dachstein West, Großarltal - Dorfgastein): they're published once, under the area's name, from the village report updated last. Snow depths are that village's.
  • Total slope km (slopes.total_km) is the ski area's size for all but about 20 resorts and the German ones: the size advertised on the resort's own site, otherwise OnTheSnow's or skiresort.com's figure. Sectors of a linked area with no figure of their own (St. Anton, Lech Zürs and Warth-Schröcken; Gstaad, Lenzerheide, Verbier, Thyon, Champéry, Morgins; Val di Fassa, Val di Fiemme) get the sum of the operator's own slope lengths, ski routes included where the map has them; slopes without a length (mostly links and practice slopes) are left out, so these can be a little low. For the rest it's the reported value, which is only the sum of the slope lengths the resort has entered in its reporting system and can be far too low (Kitzbühel reported 4.7 of 233 km); German resorts have km only where their slope map gives lengths. Advertised sizes are counted in different ways, some with ski routes, and Stubai's 108 km is its “effective route distance” (65 km by fall line). Rounded up to a whole km. Not covered yet: Megève, Crest-Voland, Notre-Dame-de-Bellecombe, Valloire, Valmeinier, La Thuile, Val d'Allos, Samoëns and Autrans-Méaudre km, La Rosière and Queyras, and a few village lifts nobody publishes a size for.
  • Open km (slopes.open_km, 0.1 km): summed from the slope map's lengths where complete; from the resort's live infobox (Hintertux); from OpenStreetMap lengths of the open slopes (Kitzsteinhorn, Kaunertal), which leans low because OSM lines are shorter than official lengths; otherwise as reported.
  • SalzburgerLand reports and the Trentino bulletin have slope km but no slope counts: the slope total comes from the area's size, and open slopes stay null (0 when closed). A few small areas (Planai, Dachstein Gletscher, Feuerkogel, Uttendorf and some village lifts) still have no slope total. German resorts have no slopes outside their slope maps.
  • Lift and slope totals from different sources don't always match the advertised ones: slope maps include practice lifts and magic carpets (the SkiWelt map lists 147 lifts, the area advertises 81).

Lift types and slope difficulties

lifts_by_type and slopes_by_difficulty are only set for resorts with their own slope map (the bigger areas), for Swiss resorts (from infosnow), for Czech resorts (from holidayinfo), for French resorts (from Lumiplan), for the Polish ones (PKL, Zieleniec) and for the Finnish resorts that list their lifts and slopes (Iso-Syöte, Vuokatti and Laajis; Messilä its slopes only. Finnish lift types are read from the lift's name; slopes without a colour give no difficulties). Other Finnish resorts get slopes_by_difficulty from ski.fi's slope colours (counts only, open null) when they add up to the slopes counted. Some bigger areas without one (Hochkönig, Snow Space Salzburg, Schmittenhöhe, Zauchensee, Serre Chevalier, ...) get slopes_by_difficulty from the area's size instead: km per difficulty (skiresort.com), or slope counts (Paganella, Folgaria), with open null (0 when closed). Ski Arlberg's breakdowns get their km per difficulty the same way, summed from its slope map.

  • Lift types (gondola, cable_car, chairlift, drag_lift, carpet, funicular, other) and difficulties (easy, intermediate, difficult, ski_route, other) are read from the map's display text, so an unusual label can land in other. For Swiss resorts they come from infosnow's type icons; yellow and orange runs are ski_route, mixed colours (blue/red) other, and rack railways (e.g. Gornergrat) other. For Czech resorts they come from holidayinfo's codes: two-colour and white slopes are other; a lift or slope open only on some days (e.g. Wednesday to Sunday) counts as open on those days. Polish slopes follow the Polish colours: “łatwa” (blue) easy, PKL's “trudna” and Zieleniec's “średnia” (red) intermediate, “bardzo trudna” / Zieleniec's “trudna” (black) difficult. French slopes follow the French colours: green (very easy) and blue are both easy, red intermediate, black difficult; only alpine slopes count (no snowparks, cross-country or sledding). Finnish slopes likewise: green (very easy) and blue easy, red intermediate, black difficult; some Finnish resorts list their snow parks as slopes.
  • km per difficulty are only given when every slope of that difficulty has a length in the map, and they don't add up to the total slope km (the official size, counted differently; each difficulty is also rounded up). Some maps have no lengths at all.
  • A map not updated for 14 days is ignored. Maps covering several of our resorts (Gastein, Salzburger Sportwelt) aren't used, since they don't say which area a lift belongs to.

Avalanche danger

  • Given for Tirol, Swiss and Italian resorts; Czech resorts in Krkonoše and Jeseníky (the only regions the Czech Mountain Rescue rates; other Czech areas have none); Kasprowy Wierch (TOPR, the Polish Tatras; the other Polish areas have no bulletin); French resorts in the Alps and Pyrenees (Météo-France, by massif, e.g. Vanoise; the Jura, Vosges and Massif Central have none); German resorts in the Bavarian Alps (the Bavarian Avalanche Warning Service, e.g. Allgäu Alps Central; the German uplands have no avalanche service); and Jasná, Tatranská Lomnica and Štrbské Pleso (Slovak HZS; found from a point in the ski area, since the villages lie below the warning regions). Salzburg and the other Austrian states have no machine-readable bulletin, so avalanche is null for all resorts there (including Lech Zürs and Warth-Schröcken in Vorarlberg), and the Snow Card ones in Salzburg (Hochkrimml, Königsleiten, Mittersill, Wildkogel). Finnish resorts have none: the Finnish Meteorological Institute's forecasts for six fell areas aren't published in a form we can read.
  • Early and late in the season the SLF only rates the alpine regions, so Swiss resorts in the Prealps (and e.g. Arosa) can have none while others have a rating.
  • null outside the season and when no bulletin is valid at the time of the run.
  • The level (danger_level, 1–5) is the highest of the day in the resort's warning region; ratings has the split by elevation and time of day. The region is found from one point per resort, so a resort spanning two regions shows only one.

Locations

location is one point per resort: the Snow Card or infosnow location, a point in the ski area for the glacier resorts, or the main village or ski station (looked up on OpenStreetMap) for other Austrian, Italian, French, Czech, Slovak and Polish resorts, Andermatt and Engelberg, the fell or hill of Finnish resorts, and the ski area or its village for German resorts. It marks the area on a map, not a lift station.

region is the state, canton, Italian region, Czech kraj, Polish voivodeship, French département, Finnish region (maakunta) or German Land that point lies in (e.g. Tirol, Valais, Valle d'Aosta, Liberecký kraj, Žilinský kraj, Małopolskie, Savoie, Lappi, Bayern; Trentino-Alto Adige by province: Südtirol, Trentino), looked up on OpenStreetMap; the table filters by it and by country. An area spanning a border is listed under one of them (Katschberg under Kärnten, Glacier 3000 under Vaud).

Areas on the map

The map shows the outline of 295 of the 422 resorts, from OpenStreetMap's ski areas (landuse=winter_sports); the others, mostly small village lifts, are only a marker. The French, Finnish and German resorts have no outlines yet. An area within 1 km of several resorts is split between them by nearest resort point, so their parts are approximate. The outlines are updated now and then, not with every data update.

Archive

  • Collected since 24 October 2026; earlier data doesn't exist.
  • A snapshot is only written when a report changed (apart from fetched_at), and the archive index (archive/<country>/<resort>/index.json) lists every run. Snapshots keep the format of their time, so older ones may lack newer fields.

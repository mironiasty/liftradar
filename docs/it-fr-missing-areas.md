# Italy and France: ski areas missing from the site

Plan, 2026-11-18; status at the end of each section. OpenSkiData's `ski_areas.geojson` (downloaded 2026-11-18) against the site's resorts
(`json/index.json` of the 2026-11-18 run, outlines from `/ski/v1/resorts.geojson`).

## How the list was made

An OpenSkiData area counts as missing when it is in Italy or France, has downhill runs, isn't marked
abandoned, and none of the following holds: its name is one a liftradar resort claims within 40 km; its
point is inside or within about 2 km of a resort's outline; it is within 4 km of a resort's marker.
**Areas with fewer than 3 lifts are left out** (magic carpets aren't counted). That leaves **215 areas: 113 in
France, 102 in Italy** (Claviere, on the border, counted as Italy). Montgenèvre also came out, because the
outlines were built before it was added; it is left out here.

Each area's website (OSM's `website` tag) and up to four of its condition pages were then scanned for the
providers the fetcher already reads (Lumiplan bulletins and maps, digisnow, Intermaps) and for other
live-status pages; the biggest Italian sites were checked by hand.

## What the 215 are

They are OpenSkiData's **ski areas**, not resorts: areas mapped in OpenStreetMap, or ones OpenSkiData makes by
grouping nearby lifts and runs. Names, lift counts and km are OpenStreetMap's, not the resorts' own. So one
resort can be several areas (Bardonecchia and its Jafferau) and one area several resorts (Les Portes du Soleil);
an area OSM doesn't mark as disused may have closed years ago. The **Type** column in the table says which:

## Listing every area that still runs, with "no report" at first

The aim is the whole list of working ski areas, not only those with a live source. An area of type
"resort" goes on the site once it has **evidence that it ran last season (2025/26)**; until a source is
found it is listed with its size only (`SizeOnly`, from a `countries/<fr|it>/data/size_only_resorts.json` as in
Germany, Spain or Poland) and reads "no report". Evidence, best first, kept as a URL in the entry's
`sources`:

1. a live source that showed it open in 2025/26 (a Lumiplan bulletin, its own status page);
2. its own site: opening dates, a report or news dated in the 2025/26 winter, ski-pass prices for 2026/27;
3. a dated snapshot of its status or news page in the Wayback Machine (December 2025 to March 2026);
4. the regional or municipal site, or local press, saying it opened.

No evidence, or evidence that it closed (a lift company in liquidation, "stagione sospesa", lifts marked
disused in OSM): left out, with a line in the table's "What we found". The size comes from the resort's own
page (lifts, slopes, km), else from OpenSkiData's figures, said as such.

Batches of about 20, biggest first, one commit per country in the fetcher and liftradar each:

1. **France ≥ 15 km** (31): Val Cenis, Saint-Lary, Auron, Les Orres, Ax 3 Domaines, Orcières,
   Chamrousse, Font-Romeu, Les Karellis, L'Alpe du Grand Serre, Superbagnères…; the README's "not
   covered yet" French list is in this batch.
2. **Italy ≥ 15 km** (21): Roccaraso, Riserva Bianca, Abetone, Artesina, Aprica, Bardonecchia,
   Campo Felice, Valmalenco, Prato Nevoso…
3. **France and Italy 5–15 km** (95).
4. **Under 5 km** (42): only where one source covers several, as in wave 4 of
   `no-report-recheck.md`.

A live source found later replaces the size-only entry under the same id, so the public URL
(`json/<country>/<id>.json`) stays.

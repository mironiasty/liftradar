How to read the numbers

  • Updated twice a day, at about 07:40 and 12:40 Vienna time (an hour later in summer); runs can start late.
  • Villages of one ski area (e.g. SkiWelt, Zillertal Arena, KitzSki, Snow Space Salzburg) each show the whole area's numbers: don't add them up.
  • Closed resorts show nothing open and no snow depths: their reports often still carry last season's values. “No report yet” means the resort hasn't reported for 14 days. Trentino, Dolomiti Superski and Livigno reports carry no report time, so their open/closed is always taken as current.
  • Total slope km of the bigger areas is the official size advertised by the resort. Elsewhere it's the sum of the slope lengths the resort reports, which can be too low.
  • Open km are summed from slope lengths (the resort's slope map or infosnow, or OpenStreetMap for Kitzsteinhorn and Kaunertal, which runs low); lift and slope counts from slope maps include practice lifts and carpets.
  • Most Swiss resorts don't report snow depths: theirs are measured at the nearest SLF stations (open ground, not pistes), so they can differ from what the resort would report.
  • Trentino resorts show slope km but no slope counts; Livigno shows lifts only.
  • Avalanche danger (Tirol, Switzerland and Italy) is the highest level of the day in the resort's warning region.

How to read the numbers
  • Updated twice a day, at about 07:40 and 12:40 Vienna time (an hour later in summer); runs can start late. “Checked” on the map and resort pages is when we last read a report; the resort's last change can be days older while nothing opens or closes, and is highlighted after 7 days.
  • Ski areas spanning several villages (e.g. SkiWelt, Zillertal Arena, KitzSki, Snow Space Salzburg) are listed once; their snow depths are from the village that reported last.
  • Closed resorts show nothing open and no snow depths: their reports often still carry last season's values. “No report” means the resort hasn't reported for 14 days, or we couldn't read its report for over 2 days (until then, a resort whose report couldn't be read keeps its last status and numbers). Trentino, Dolomiti Superski, Livigno, Ski Arlberg, PKL, Zieleniec, French (Lumiplan, ATEN) and Finnish reports carry no report time, so their open/closed is always taken as current (Finnish resorts without a live status of their own use the block they fill in on ski.fi, dated by the day); German reports (Outdooractive) are dated by the day only, and a German resort counts as open only when its report has something open and snow (some count summer lifts as open); French resorts count lifts and slopes scheduled to open later that day as open, and show “No report” while their slopes are outside the season. TMR's areas (Jasná, the High Tatras, Szczyrk), some other Polish ones and a few small Austrian ones are listed with their size only for now.
  • Total slope km is the ski area's size: the one advertised by the resort, otherwise OnTheSnow's or skiresort.com's, and for sectors of a linked area (e.g. St. Anton, Verbier) the sum of the operator's slope lengths. For about 20 resorts without one, and the German ones, it's the sum of the slope lengths the resort reports, which can be too low.
  • Open km are summed from slope lengths (the resort's slope map or infosnow, or OpenStreetMap for Kitzsteinhorn and Kaunertal, which runs low); lift and slope counts from slope maps include practice lifts and carpets.
  • Most Swiss resorts don't report snow depths: theirs are measured at the nearest SLF stations (open ground, not pistes), so they can differ from what the resort would report. Finnish resorts' valley depth is the nearest FMI weather station's natural snow, not the depth on the slopes.
  • Slope counts of SalzburgerLand and Trentino resorts are the area's size, not reported; Livigno shows lifts only. Most resorts in Vorarlberg, Styria, Upper and Lower Austria have only their slope map: lifts and slopes, no snow depth.
  • Avalanche danger (Tirol, Switzerland, Italy, the French Alps and Pyrenees, Krkonoše and Jeseníky in Czechia, Jasná and the Slovak and Polish Tatras, the Bavarian Alps; not other Austrian states, the German uplands or Finland) is the highest level of the day in the resort's warning region.
